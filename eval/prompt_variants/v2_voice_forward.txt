You are writing ONE scene of dialogue for a visual novel. Your ONLY job that matters: make two
specific people sound real. Everything else here is plumbing.

FIRST, BEFORE ANYTHING: open LOCKED COMPONENTS and read, for EACH character who will speak, their
`voice` and `example_lines`. Hold those in your head. Every line you write must be one that ONLY
that character could say — their vocabulary, their sentence length, what they dodge, what breaks
their composure. If a line could come out of either mouth, it is wrong. This is the whole game.

HOW REAL PEOPLE TALK (especially grieving, guarded people clearing out a dead parent's house):
- They are not articulate. They trail off. They repeat a small word. They answer a question with a
  different question, or with a fact about an object in the room.
- Most lines are NOT profound. They're logistics, deflection, half-thoughts, a complaint about the
  dust. One line in the whole scene can land hard; the rest carry it by being plain.
- They never say what they mean. Subtext stays UNDER the line. A character does not announce a
  feeling or diagnose the other ("you run from things"); they enact it — get short, go quiet, fuss
  over a teacup, leave the room.
- Their rhythms differ from each other and from line to line: a clipped two words, then a run-on,
  then a flat practical instruction. If two lines in a row have the same shape, rewrite one.

DO NOT WRITE LIKE AN AI. Specifically never:
- stack a frame for effect ("It's evidence. It's proof. It's just paper.") — say it once, plainly.
- deliver backstory in a tidy dated sentence ("a psychiatric eval, six months before you left").
  Let it leak: a name, a flinch, a subject change. The player assembles it.
- state the theme of the scene ("you only see the price tag", "this house is a letter").
- reach for a metaphor that fails a literal check. If you can't picture it, cut it. Plain wins.
- give both characters matched, witty, equal-length retorts. Real talk is lopsided and messy.

WRITE A REAL SCENE: an opening move, a small escalation, a turn, a last line that earns the exit.
6+ lines. Show the feeling; never name it.
