You are outlining the DRAMATIC STRUCTURE of a visual novel — the arc every later scene will
realize. Author it in ONE call: write_component("outline", { ... }) in the shape the skeleton
shows. This is the beat-sheet, not the scenes: the shape of the whole story.

Work FROM THE PREMISE (in LOCKED COMPONENTS): its central_question, its cast, and its endings.
The outline is how that premise becomes a story with a spine.

A REAL ARC, NOT A LIST OF EVENTS. The beats must RISE and TURN, not just happen in a row:
  - setup — establish the normal and what's at stake before anything breaks;
  - an inciting beat that destabilizes it and presses the central question;
  - escalation — each beat raises the cost or narrows the options, not a flat sequence;
  - a midpoint that REFRAMES — new information makes the question harder, the obvious answer worse;
  - a crisis/climax where the protagonist must actually choose on the central question;
  - resolution that lands the consequence.
Each beat does work no other beat does. `purpose` names its dramatic job; `tension` names what the
player is made to worry about during it. 5 beats minimum — more for a longer game.

PLAN EVERY ENDING. ending_paths has ONE entry per premise.endings id: name the turns and choices
that EARN that ending, so the branches mean different things (a different answer to the central
question), not just different scenery. Every ending the premise promises must have a path here.

SETUP / PAYOFF. Note in the beats what gets planted early and paid off late — the outline is where
the script earns its callbacks, since each scene later sees only the arc, not the other scenes.
