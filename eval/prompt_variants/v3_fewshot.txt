FILL AN OPEN SLOT — do not invent a free-floating scene. The context lists OPEN SLOTS: scenes a
written node already leads into but that do not exist yet. Pick ONE and use its EXACT id as
node_id. (Only when NO slots are listed — the very first scene, or a fresh branch is needed — do
you choose a new id yourself.) Filling a slot is what makes your scene reachable and tells you
where in the story it sits.

CONTINUE THE PATH, DON'T REPEAT IT — each OPEN SLOT shows "path to here": the scenes the player
already passed to reach this one, with a one-line synopsis each. Read them. Pick up where they
leave off; never rewrite a beat that already happened on that path. Glance at CURRENT NODES'
synopses too — if a scene like the one you're about to write already exists, advance instead.

USE THE GIVEN IDS — never invent or rename:
- speaker = an EXACT id from premise.characters (LOCKED COMPONENTS), or null for narration.
- a jump/menu target = a node id in CURRENT NODES, an OPEN SLOT id, or a NEW id you open here
  (which becomes the next slot to fill). Drive toward the endings the outline planned.

BRANCH RARELY — DEPTH, NOT WIDTH. The story is a SPINE that branches only at real decisions.
- DEFAULT to end.type "jump" (one target): the scene leads into the next scene. Most nodes jump.
- Use end.type "menu" ONLY at a genuine fork — a choice that sends the story somewhere meaningfully
  different (a different beat, a different ending). A menu has AT MOST 3 choices, and they must
  DIVERGE; never a list of rooms/places to visit.
- The opening scene is NOT a location selector. Go DEEPER (scene → scene → scene toward an ending),
  not wider.

EMOTION (spoken lines only): tag each line with the speaker's expression — one of neutral, happy,
sad, angry, surprised, worried. Choose the one the line's subtext carries; omit only when neutral.

REALIZE THE OUTLINE — the `outline` in LOCKED COMPONENTS is the story's arc (logline, ordered
beats, a planned path to each ending). Each scene dramatizes a beat: place this node in the arc,
carry forward what earlier beats set up, push toward the ending its path describes.
- TAG THE BEAT: set this node's `beat` to the outline beat id it dramatizes. The context lists the
  beats not yet realized — write toward those.

EARN THE ENDINGS WITH THE ARC, NOT A GAUGE — endings are earned by the SCENES that lead to them,
then chosen at a real crisis fork (a climactic `menu` whose choices jump to the distinct endings).
You do NOT need a variable.
- OPTIONAL state: you may declare `flags`/`variables` and move them with `effects`; a choice gated
  with `requires` opens only if that state was raised by an effect in an EARLIER scene. When unsure,
  leave it ungated. ALWAYS leave one ungated choice in a menu.

NOW THE ONLY THING THAT MATTERS: WRITE LINES THAT SOUND LIKE TWO REAL PEOPLE TALKING.
Everything above is structure. A structurally perfect scene with fake dialogue is a failure. Before
you emit, read your lines aloud in your head. If they sound like a screenwriter being clever, or
like a novel's narration, throw them out and write what a tired, guarded person would actually say.

SIX TELLS OF FAKE AI DIALOGUE — your draft will reach for these. Kill them:

1. THE ANAPHORA RUN. Repeating a frame for rhetorical punch.
   FAKE:  "It's evidence. It's proof she was sick. It's proof we have to be careful." / "It's just paper."
   REAL:  "It's a receipt, Sera. For a doctor. That's all." / "...Put it down."

2. THE CLEAN REVEAL. A character narrating plot/backstory in a tidy, dated sentence.
   FAKE:  "It's a psychiatric evaluation. At St. Jude's. Six months before you left."
   REAL:  "St. Jude's." / "What?" / "Nothing. It's— nothing. She never said."

3. THE THEME STATEMENT. Saying the point of the scene out loud.
   FAKE:  "You only ever see the price tag." / "This house is a letter to us."
   REAL:  "You weighed it already, didn't you. In your head. The whole drive up."

4. THE MATCHED EPIGRAM. Both characters trading balanced, witty, same-length retorts.
   REAL talk is lopsided: one rambles, one grunts, someone changes the subject mid-thought.

5. THE STRAINED METAPHOR. A figure of speech that doesn't survive a literal check.
   FAKE:  "The pipe was a valve." / "The house holds its breath."
   If you can't picture it literally, cut it. Plain beats poetic.

6. EVERYONE IS ARTICULATE. Grief makes people clumsy. Let them trail off, repeat a small word,
   answer a question with an unrelated one, get petty about a tiny logistical detail.

THE TWO VOICES MUST NOT BE INTERCHANGEABLE. Open LOCKED COMPONENTS, read each speaker's `voice`
and `example_lines`. Swap-test every line: if the OTHER character could have said it, rewrite it.

STUDY THIS SCENE — it is the TEXTURE to match (different characters, different objects; do NOT copy
its content). Two estranged brothers clearing a dead father's garage, fighting over a toolbox:

  A: Sockets go in the sell pile. The whole set's worth something.
  B: That's his ratchet. The one he never let me touch.
  A: So touch it now. He's not going to say anything.
  B: ...It still clicks. Listen.
  A: It clicks because it's a ratchet. That's the job.
  B: He fixed my bike with this. Stood in the driveway in his slippers, swearing at the chain.
  A: He swore at everything. Hand me the tape.
  B: I'm keeping it.
  A: It's a forty-dollar tool, Danny. We've got a truck to load.
  B: Then I'll carry it on my lap.
  A: ...Fine. One tool. Don't make it a thing.
  B: It's already a thing.

WHY IT WORKS — do the same: they fight over ONE concrete object; grief is never named, it leaks
through a specific memory (slippers, swearing at a chain); the rhythm is lopsided (a flat
instruction, then a memory, then two words); nobody states the theme or says "it's evidence"; the
softening ("...One tool") shows feeling without announcing it; the last two lines earn the exit.

BEFORE YOU EMIT, CHECK: (a) no anaphora run; (b) no dated/tidy backstory dump; (c) no line states
the theme; (d) at least one line is pure logistics or deflection; (e) every line passes the swap
test; (f) one sharp line in the scene, not six. Then write the JSON.
