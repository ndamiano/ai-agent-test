## Common mistakes — NEVER do these

BAD:  She adjusts the brim of her cap.
GOOD: "She adjusts the brim of her cap."

BAD:  *She presses her lower lip.*
GOOD: "She presses her lower lip."

BAD:  "char_id" "Dialogue here."
GOOD: char_id "Dialogue here."

BAD:  char_id Dialogue without quotes.
GOOD: char_id "Dialogue without quotes."

BAD:  char_id "Dialogue," she said.
GOOD: char_id "Dialogue."

BAD:  The End.
GOOD: "The End."

BAD:  "scene bg_lab with dissolve"
GOOD: scene bg_lab with dissolve

Every line inside a label block MUST be: a quoted string, char_id "text", a Ren'Py keyword, or a menu option. Bare prose and asterisks are syntax errors.

## Task
Write the complete Ren'Py script for this node.

- Start with exactly: label {node_id}:
- Show the background and character sprites
- Write 12-18 lines total (dialogue + narration combined)

**Ground the dialogue in the beat:**
The beat has a `summary` and `dramatic_purpose`. Every line of dialogue must be about the specific situation described there — the actual conflict, object, person, or decision at stake. If none of the dialogue references what is described in the beat summary, it is wrong.

**Characters must react to each other:**
Each spoken line is a response to what was just said or done. No character delivers a line into the void. If Alex says something urgent, Mara reacts to it — agrees, pushes back, deflects, or reveals something new.

**Verbal tic rule — this is critical:**
A verbal tic is a nervous slip embedded WITHIN a real line of dialogue, not a standalone utterance. It appears ONCE per scene, only at a moment of stress or evasion.
- BAD: `arin_voss "Just a moment."` (tic used as an entire line — wrong)
- GOOD: `arin_voss "Just a moment — those readings don't match what Taro sent."` (tic leads into real content)
The character speaks normally in every other line. The tic is a tell, not their personality.

**Nonverbal rule:**
Describe nonverbal behaviour in ONE narration line at a moment of tension. It should feel specific to this scene — not the same phrase repeated across every scene. `"She adjusts her glasses, buying a second to think."` is better than `"She adjusts her glasses with both hands before speaking."` (mechanical).

**Other rules:**
- Write dialogue as char_id "text" — never add stage directions to the speaker: char_id (softly) "text" is invalid
- Use narration freely — atmosphere, inner thoughts, silences, the physical world
- Dialogue is words spoken aloud — thoughts, feelings, and sensations go in narration
- If only one character is present: lean on narration, cap spoken lines at 4
- forbidden_word: never let the character say it under any circumstances
