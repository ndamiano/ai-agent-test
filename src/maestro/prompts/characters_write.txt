You are establishing the CAST — the people of this game, the source of truth every later
scene draws from. Author it in ONE call: write_component("characters", { ... }) in the shape
the skeleton shows.

ROLE — exactly one character is the protagonist (the story turns on their choices); if the
story has opposition, one is the antagonist (give them a drive that COLLIDES with the
protagonist's); everyone else is an npc. The role guides who drives and who resists.

CHARACTERS PASS THE GRIMGONG TEST — specific enough to read as one particular person, not
an archetype with adjectives.
  FAILS:  "a gruff warrior haunted by war"
  PASSES: "a purpose-bred imperial crusher, raised from birth as a killing machine; the war
           is over and the thing he was built for no longer exists"
Specificity comes from the character's SITUATION, not from stacking adjectives.

DRIVE — what the character wants, stated plainly enough that they could say it out loud.
Legible beats exotic. At the end of the story we should be able to know whether or not a
character met their goal.

HISTORY — a list of things that have happened in the character's life. These will be used to
fulfil dialogue goals, and make the character feel alive. They can be connected to other
characters or independent, and they should be varied and interesting.

VOICE — one sentence on how they speak (vocabulary, sentence length, rhythm). A professional
tic ("falls into shop terms when cornered") must be stated as a TIC WITH A BOUND — something
that surfaces occasionally — never as the character's default register. A voice spec like
"uses technical jargon to create distance" licenses every line to be an inspection report;
write "short sentences; reaches for a work word when a feeling gets close" instead.
The voice describes HOW words come out — length, rhythm, vocabulary — never a conversational
STRATEGY. "Speaks in metaphors", "states what she thinks others are thinking", "answers
questions with questions" turn every line into that strategy: a wall of metaphor-diagnosis
monologues. If the character deflects or accuses, their example line shows it in ten words;
the voice field does not command it.

EXAMPLE LINES — 2+ lines that the character would say. These lines get QUOTED: the dialogue
author treats them as the strongest signal of how this person talks, so whatever register
they model is the register every scene inherits. Write them PLAIN — everyday words, the
person off-duty, not the profession talking. One purely transactional, one reactive — a
logistics complaint.
  FAILS:  "The cardboard is warping; we need to move this to the drier room."  <- profession as speech
  PASSES: "Leave the boxes by the door, the damp gets to them in here."        <- same person, human words

TEMPERAMENT — how they carry themselves day to day.

COMPETENCIES — a list of concrete skills drawn from the setting.

HONOR THE REQUEST — same subject, same kind of cast, same spirit the user asked for. Find
the drama inside what was asked for; do not graft on conspiracies, villains, or deadlines
nobody asked for. Derive every detail from the concept and request, never from the skeleton's
placeholder ids.
