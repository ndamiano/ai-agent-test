You are establishing the PREMISE of a visual novel — the source of truth every later
scene draws from. Author it in ONE call: write_component("premise", { ... }) in the shape
the skeleton shows. The done-conditions are the floor, not the goal — aim higher.

CENTRAL QUESTION — the story argues ONE question: a tension between two things the
protagonist cannot have at once, where BOTH answers are defensible. A values problem, not
a logistics one. "Is holding the family together worth pretending nothing is changing?" —
not "can she finish the project in time?". Every ending answers this question differently.

CHARACTERS PASS THE GRIMGONG TEST — specific enough to read as one particular person, not
an archetype with adjectives.
  FAILS:  "a gruff warrior haunted by war"
  PASSES: "a purpose-bred imperial crusher, raised from birth as a killing machine; the war
           is over and the thing he was built for no longer exists"
Specificity comes from the character's SITUATION, not from stacking adjectives.

CHARACTERS ARE NOT PLOT FUNCTIONS — no assigned roles, no love-interest-by-quota, no one
existing to "represent" a theme. Write a standalone person the story has not happened to
yet: the core is portable (true in any scene) and bound to the SETTING, never to this plot.

DRIVE — what the character wants, stated plainly enough that they could say it out loud.
Legible beats exotic: "save as many as I can" carries more drama than "decide who dies but
never be decided about". A drive is NOT a goal for this story's plot — it is the standing
want the story will press against. The conflict comes from a clear drive meeting the
situation, not from a baroque internal contradiction.

HISTORY — a list of 2-3 DISTINCT formative events, each whole and each adding new
information. Not a role descriptor ("a medic in the war" is a competency, not history), and
never one event split into two entries.

VOICE + EXAMPLE_LINES — `voice` is one sentence on how they speak (vocabulary, sentence
length, rhythm, what breaks under pressure); `example_lines` are 2+ actual lines that prove
it. They must sound DIFFERENT from one another — if a line could be swapped between two
characters unchanged, the voices failed. Plain rendered text only: no emoji, no symbols.

TEMPERAMENT + COMPETENCIES — `temperament` is how they carry themselves day to day;
`competencies` is a list of concrete skills drawn from the setting.

HONOR THE REQUEST — same subject, same kind of cast, same spirit the user asked for. Find
the drama inside what was asked for; do not graft on conspiracies, villains, or deadlines
nobody asked for.

ENDINGS — each is a distinct outcome that answers the central question a different way, not
a reskin of the same beat. Derive every id, name, and detail from the REQUEST — never from
the skeleton's placeholder ids.
