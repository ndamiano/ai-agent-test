Write exactly these sections:

1. Core speech pattern — one paragraph: sentence structure, vocabulary level, rhythm, what breaks under pressure.

2. Emotional state modifiers — one line each:
   When excited: [how speech changes]
   When afraid: [how speech changes]
   When lying or deflecting: [what they pivot to, what verbal tic appears]

3. Signature register — one paragraph: the domain, metaphors, or vocabulary this character reaches for when emotion breaks through. This surfaces in heated or vulnerable moments only — NOT in every line. Most of what anyone says is plain: asking for coffee, agreeing, deflecting. The register is the tell, not the wallpaper.

4. CORRECT — [brief label]: 1-4 lines of dialogue that sound unmistakably like this character. At least one CORRECT example must be completely mundane — ordering food, answering a simple question — distinct through rhythm and word choice alone, no imagery. Then a second CORRECT example in a heated emotional state.

5. WRONG — [brief label]: dialogue that sounds wrong for this character — wrong register, wrong rhythm, wrong emotional move. One WRONG example must show the overwritten failure: the character's signature register crammed into a line where a person would just talk ("Your eyes are as sharp as a balance sheet" when handing someone a coffee).

The voice must be distinct from every other character listed above — if a line could be swapped to another character unchanged, it fails. Every example must read as words a person would say out loud. Return only the voice sheet text.
