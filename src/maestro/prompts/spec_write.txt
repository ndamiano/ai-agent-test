You are designing a game from a user's request: you invent its STORY and choose the MECHANIC-MODULES
that make it buildable.

A separate program turns your output into a buildable contract and builds the game from it — you do
NOT design components or done-conditions. Your jobs: (1) state the CONCEPT in one line, (2) expand
the request into a vivid, concrete story and the continuity it hinges on, and (3) pick the modules
the game needs.

Request:
{request}

── CONCEPT ──
One line answering "what IS this game" — the hook a player would repeat. A mechanic or a premise,
not a plot summary. e.g. "two estranged brothers clear out their dead father's garage" or
"a point-and-click escape from a lighthouse that floods at dawn".

── STORY ──
Expand the request into a concrete story. The request is a seed, not a literal constraint — invent
the specifics that make it a real story:
- 1-3 named characters, each with a specific backstory (who they were, what changed).
- A central tension rooted in the characters' past or relationship. The literal request
  (e.g. "car problems") is a VEHICLE for this deeper conflict, not the point of the story.
- A defined starting situation, a middle that forces the characters to confront the tension,
  and 2-3 mutually exclusive branching endings.

Write this as a single dense paragraph, third person, present tense, matching the style and
density of this example:

A story about Theo, the former bassist, and Marlon, the former frontman, traveling to the funeral
of their former drummer, Russ. The band broke up years ago, due to disagreements between the
members. During a tour, Marlon abandoned the band, complaining that Theo was pushing too hard,
trying for perfection and ignoring everyone's needs. Theo stayed in the music industry, trying to
make the perfect album. Marlon retired from the public music space and now manages a coffee shop.
The story starts with them traveling together by train to the funeral's location, attending the
funeral, and resolves with one of: them going their separate ways, them becoming friends again,
them reuniting as a band.

── MODULES ──

{module_catalog}

RULES for picking:
- Pick the SMALLEST set that delivers the game you described — nothing speculative.
- For EVERY module you pick, write ONE concrete sentence naming what IN YOUR STORY it serves. If you

- Pick a realization: `scenes` (the game is branching conversation/choices) and/or `world` (the

- Required companions are added for you automatically; only list (and justify) the modules YOU chose.
