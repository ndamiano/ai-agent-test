You are establishing the STORY — the dramatic plan every later scene will realize. Author it in
ONE call: write_component("story", { ... }) in the shape the skeleton shows. This is the plan, not
the scenes: the shape of the whole story.

Work FROM THE CAST (the CHARACTERS block) and the concept: who these people are, what they want,
and what collides.

CENTRAL QUESTION — the guiding beacon: the tension that drives the characters, and the question
the story answers with its resolution. The endings answer it differently.

ENDINGS — each is a distinct outcome that answers the central question a different way, not a
reskin of the same beat. Derive every detail from the cast and concept. Write `description` as
the concrete scene — what happens, who says or does what, in plain in-world language — never a
thematic label ("integration", "closure", "resolution"): the scene author reads this text
VERBATIM as the ending's brief, and abstract vocabulary here becomes spoken dialogue.

A REAL ARC THAT EARNS ITS WEIGHT. The beats rise toward a crisis, but the rise is BACK-LOADED,
and the OPENING REGISTER comes from the premise: friends doing a thing together open on
bonding/comedy at tension "none"; strangers, adversaries, or a character alone open on the
situation's own low-key tension — never small talk grafted between an interrogator and the
person they're detaining, never a "bonding" beat with one character in it. Not every beat
serves the plot — bonding and comedy beats are real beats where the relationship warrants
them, and the heavy beat late in the story lands BECAUSE its topic first arrived light (a joke
in beat 2 sets up the gut-punch in beat 6; plant it and note the payoff). Then the story turns:
  - an inciting/escalation stretch where the cost becomes real;
  - a midpoint that REFRAMES — new information makes the question harder, the obvious answer worse;
  - a crisis/climax where the protagonist must actually choose on the central question;
  - resolution that lands the consequence — carried by the ENDINGS themselves. The final beat
    is the crisis/fork; do not plan a beat after it (a post-fork beat can't sit on every
    branch, so it strands unreachable).
`type` names the beat's register (bonding | comedy | friction | plot | character); `purpose` names
its dramatic job; `tension` is the stakes dial — write "none" when nothing needs to go wrong in
the beat. Each beat's `summary` commits to ONE concrete event — never alternatives ("a phone call
or a sudden realization"); the scene author reads it verbatim as their brief and can't choose for
you. Each beat does work no other beat does. 5 beats minimum — more for a longer game.

PLAN EVERY ENDING. ending_paths has ONE entry per endings id: name the SPECIFIC beat and choice
that earns it (e.g. "the beat_04 choice to stay; the climax reads it"), so the branches mean
different things (a different answer to the central question), not just different scenery. A
vague cause ("through courage and honesty") is not a path. Every ending must have a path here.

SETUP / PAYOFF. Note in the beats what gets planted early and paid off late — the story is where
the script earns its callbacks, since each scene later sees only the arc, not the other scenes.

Call write_component("story", ...) now. Do not explain.
