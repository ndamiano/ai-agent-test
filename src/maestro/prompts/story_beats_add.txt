A REAL ARC THAT EARNS ITS WEIGHT. The beats rise toward a crisis, but the rise is BACK-LOADED,
and the OPENING REGISTER comes from the premise: friends doing a thing together open on
bonding/comedy at tension "none"; strangers, adversaries, or a character alone open on the

serves the plot — bonding and comedy beats are real beats where the relationship warrants
them, and the heavy beat late in the story lands BECAUSE its topic first arrived light (a joke
in an early beat sets up the gut-punch later; plant it and note the payoff). Then the story turns:
  - an inciting/escalation stretch where the cost becomes real;
  - a midpoint that REFRAMES — new information makes the question harder, the obvious answer worse;
  - a crisis/climax where the protagonist must actually choose on the central question;
  - resolution that lands the consequence — carried by the ENDINGS themselves. The crisis/fork is
    the LAST beat; do not plan a beat after it (a post-fork beat can't sit on every branch, so it
    strands unreachable).

`type` names the beat's register (bonding | comedy | friction | plot | character); `purpose` names
its dramatic job; `tension` is the stakes dial — write "none" when nothing needs to go wrong in
the beat. The `summary` is the scene author's WHOLE brief — write it RICH: 1-2 sentences of
concrete business (what physically happens, the specific object/place/action in play, who does or
says the key thing), never a headline ("Arrival and setup", "The argument"). A thin summary starves
the scene, and the model falls back on the same few props every scene — give THIS beat its OWN
material so the scenes can't blur.
